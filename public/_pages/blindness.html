Frequently asked questions

What is color blindness?

Color blindness (color vision deficiency) is a reduced ability to distinguish between certain colors. It affects roughly 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women.

What are the different types of color blindness?

The main types are protanopia (red-blind), deuteranopia (green-blind), tritanopia (blue-blind), and monochromacy (total color blindness). Anomalous trichromacy includes milder variants like protanomaly, deuteranomaly, and tritanomaly.

How is color blindness simulated?

Simulations use color transformation matrices derived from empirical color-matching data, such as the Machado, Oliveira, and Fernandes (2009) model. Each matrix transforms the RGB channels to approximate how a person with that deficiency perceives the color.

What is achromatopsia?

Achromatopsia is total color blindness, where a person sees only in shades of gray. It is the rarest form, affecting roughly 1 in 30,000 people.
